# Thermal Management Subsystem — Technology Survey

This survey consolidates open literature and internal findings for the two-phase thermal management subsystem.

## Scope and Method

The survey covers hardware, materials, and control approaches published since 2015.
Searches were run across four databases using a fixed query set.
Inclusion required peer-reviewed publication or an internal test report.
Two reviewers screened the abstracts independently.
Full texts were extracted into the reference database for annotation.
The method follows the group's standard evidence-review procedure.

## Prior Art Survey

Prior survey work examined silicon carbide heat exchangers for compact avionics cooling loops.
Aluminum nitride substrates were evaluated for thermal conductivity under steady-state conditions.
The braze joint program compared nickel-based filler metals for stainless assemblies.
Computational fluid dynamics runs characterized the cold-plate geometry at nominal flow rates.
A fatigue study of the mounting brackets covered 10,000 duty cycles without failure.
The electromagnetic compatibility campaign measured radiated emissions from the drive electronics.
Wire-harness routing studies addressed abrasion resistance in the aft bay.
Corrosion testing of the seawater pump impeller followed the ASTM salt-spray protocol.
The software team documented the built-in test sequences for the power distribution unit.
Optical inspection of the lens assembly verified surface figure error within tolerance.
Vibration profiling of the avionics rack reproduced the launch environment specification.
The battery team assessed lithium-ion cell aging under elevated temperature storage.
Manufacturing studies compared filament-winding and autoclave cure cycles for the fuselage shell.
The navigation team calibrated the inertial measurement unit on a precision rate table.
Acoustic measurements of the compressor inlet established the noise baseline for the airframe.
The power electronics group reviewed silicon carbide device switching losses at full load.
Thermal vacuum cycling of the payload enclosure covered the planned orbital temperature range.
The structures group validated the finite element mesh against strain-gauge readings.
Lubricant selection studies for the gimbal bearings considered vacuum outgassing constraints.
The reliability team estimated mean time between failures for the cooling pump assembly.
The original basic principles of loop-heat-pipe capillary pumping were observed and recorded in the foundational experiment report from 1998.

## Program Findings

The application concept for a pumped two-phase avionics cooler was formulated in a trade study, and the feasibility experiment results were recorded.

The analytical model of the loop conductance was experimentally verified against calorimeter data, and the verification results were recorded.

The underlying boiling-curve phenomenon was characterized experimentally, and the data were recorded in the thermal physics notebook.
